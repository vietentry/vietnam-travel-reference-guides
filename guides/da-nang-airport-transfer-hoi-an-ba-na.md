# Da Nang Airport Transfer Guide: Best Ways to Reach Hoi An Old Town and Ba Na Hills (2026)

Hoi An has no airport of its own. Essentially every visitor arrives through **Da Nang
International Airport (DAD)** and covers the last **30 km by road**, a drive of **about 45
minutes** along the coastal highway. Ba Na Hills sits in the opposite direction, inland and
uphill, roughly 35 km west of the airport.

That part is simple. What is worth planning is *which* transfer you take, because most
international flights into Da Nang land late in the evening or in the small hours, and the
options narrow considerably after midnight.

---

## 1. Where the Airport Sits

Da Nang International Airport is unusual among major Asian airports: it is inside the city,
not an hour outside it. The international terminal is **Terminal 2**, purpose-built for the
APEC Summit, with short walking distances and comparatively fast immigration clearance —
typically **15 to 25 minutes** through passport control.

That location is why the airport works as a hub for the whole central region:

| Destination | Direction | Approximate distance |
|---|---|---|
| Da Nang city centre / My Khe Beach | East, coastal | A short city drive |
| Hoi An Old Town | South along the coast | 30 km, about 45 minutes |
| Ba Na Hills / Golden Bridge | West, inland and uphill | About 35 km |
| Marble Mountains (Ngu Hanh Son) | South, en route to Hoi An | On the Hoi An road |

The Hoi An road runs past My Khe Beach and the Marble Mountains, which is why many travellers
build a stop into the transfer rather than treating it as dead time.

## 2. The Four Ways to Reach Hoi An, Compared

| Option | Roughly | Meets you? | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-booked private transfer** | $15 – $25 USD | Yes — named greeter in the arrivals hall | Late arrivals, families, first visit, anyone with luggage |
| **Airport taxi rank** | Metered | No — you queue at the rank | Travellers comfortable negotiating on arrival |
| **Grab (ride-hailing app)** | Varies by demand | No — you walk to the pickup area | Solo travellers with a working SIM on landing |
| **Shared shuttle** | Cheapest per seat | Depends on operator | Budget travel, daytime, no fixed schedule pressure |

A caution on prices: the figures above are **indicative planning ranges, not quotes**. Fares
move with fuel costs, season and time of day, and a 01:00 arrival is not priced like a 14:00
arrival.

### Pre-booked private transfer

The strongest option if you are arriving tired, arriving late, or arriving with children and
suitcases. Someone is standing in the arrivals hall with your name, the price is agreed
before you land, and there is no airport surcharge to argue about at 1am. It is also the only
option on this list that does not depend on you having working mobile data the moment you
clear customs.

### Airport taxi

Reliable and always available, but metered — so the cost of the same journey varies, and a
late-night run to Hoi An will not be the cheapest way to do it. Insist on the meter.

### Grab

Convenient and transparent *if* your phone is already online. That is the catch: you need a
working eSIM or tourist SIM before you can book, and the pickup point is a walk from the
terminal rather than a greeter at the door. Surge pricing at peak hours is real.

### Shared shuttle

The cheapest per seat, but you wait while the vehicle fills, and drop-off is usually at fixed
points rather than your hotel door. Fine in daylight when your schedule is loose; poor value
in terms of time when you land after midnight.

## 3. Getting to Ba Na Hills

Ba Na Hills is a different journey from Hoi An, and treating them as interchangeable is a
common planning error. Hoi An is a flat coastal run south; Ba Na is inland and climbs into
the hills west of the city, ending at the cable car station.

Two practical points:

- **Ba Na is a day trip, not a transfer.** Most visitors base themselves in Da Nang or Hoi An
  and go up for the day, which means you want a vehicle that waits for you, or a return
  arrangement. A one-way drop at the cable car station leaves you solving the return at
  closing time along with everyone else.
- **Ride-hailing is thin on the return leg.** Getting up there is easy. Getting back down in
  the late afternoon, when the cable car empties, is where people wait longest.

If you are combining both, the itinerary that works is: airport → Hoi An (base) → Ba Na day
trip with a waiting vehicle → return.

## 4. Arriving Late at Night

Most long-haul routings put you on the ground in Da Nang at an awkward hour. What changes
after midnight:

- **The shuttle option effectively disappears.** Scheduled shared services do not run through
  the small hours.
- **Ride-hailing supply thins out**, and the wait grows exactly when you least want it.
- **The taxi rank still operates**, but it is the moment when tired travellers accept the
  first number they are given.
- **A pre-booked car is unaffected**, because the driver is already scheduled for your flight
  number and waits if you are delayed.

This is the single strongest argument for booking the transfer before you fly rather than
solving it in the arrivals hall.

---

## 5. A Short Arrival Sequence

1. **Land at Terminal 2** and follow the international arrivals corridor to immigration.
2. **Clear immigration** — present your passport with your printed eVisa and complete the
   facial scan. Budget 15–25 minutes at normal times.
3. **Collect baggage**, then choose the green or red customs channel.
4. **Pick up connectivity** at the SIM / eSIM counter in the arrivals hall if you have not
   already arranged an eSIM before flying. Do this *before* you try to book a Grab.
5. **Meet your driver** at the pickup area, or head to the taxi rank.
6. **Drive south to Hoi An** — 30 km, about 45 minutes.

## 6. What to Have Ready Before You Land

- Your **passport and printed eVisa on A4**, together, in hand luggage.
- Your **hotel address in Vietnamese**, screenshotted. It removes all ambiguity at the drop-off.
- **Connectivity sorted in advance** if you intend to rely on a ride-hailing app.
- **Your driver's contact details** if you pre-booked, saved offline.
- A little **cash in Vietnamese dong** for tolls, tips and roadside stops.

## 7. Choosing by Traveller Type

The "best" transfer depends less on price than on what your arrival actually looks like.

**Families with children.** Pre-booked private car, without much debate. Car seats are not a
given in Vietnam, luggage plus a pushchair does not fit a small sedan, and a greeter with your
name removes the negotiation step entirely at the point where children are least patient.

**Couples on a first visit.** Private transfer for the arrival, then local taxis and
ride-hailing once you have a SIM and a feel for the area. The airport run is the leg where
being met is worth most.

**Solo budget travellers.** Grab is genuinely good value in daylight, provided you sort
connectivity first. If you land after midnight, take the taxi rank rather than standing in an
empty pickup area watching prices climb.

**Groups of four or more.** Compare per-person, not per-vehicle. A single private car split
four ways often lands close to the shuttle fare while going door to door.

**Anyone with an early tour the next morning.** Pay for certainty. A late arrival plus an
unsolved transfer is how people miss the thing they flew in for.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**How long is the drive from Da Nang airport to Hoi An?**
About 45 minutes for the 30 km, along the coastal highway. Allow longer in heavy rain or at
the end of a public holiday weekend.

**Can I stop at the Marble Mountains on the way?**
Yes — Ngu Hanh Son sits on the Hoi An road. A private transfer can build in the stop; a
metered taxi or a shuttle generally cannot.

**Is there a public bus?**
There is public transport in the area, but it is not a practical airport-to-Hoi An solution
with luggage or after dark. Treat it as a local option once you are settled, not an arrival
plan.

**Should I book the transfer before I fly?**
If you land late, travel with family, or have a fixed commitment the next morning: yes. If
you land in daylight and travel light: you have options on arrival.

**Do I need Vietnamese dong on arrival?**
Some. Pre-booked transfers are usually settled in advance, but tolls, drinks and tips are
easier with a little local cash than with a card.
