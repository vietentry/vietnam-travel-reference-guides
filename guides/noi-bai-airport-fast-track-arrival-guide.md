# Noi Bai Airport (HAN) Arrival: How to Use Fast-Track Immigration and Skip the Wait (2026)

*Photo: Noi Bai International Airport Terminal 2, by Christakis Mina, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons.*

Noi Bai International Airport sits roughly **30 kilometres north of Hanoi's Old Quarter** and
is northern Vietnam's primary international gateway. For most arriving travellers it works
smoothly. For some — a business delegation landing at 01:00, a family with small children
after a fourteen-hour flight, an executive with a meeting three hours after touchdown — the
difference between a normal arrival and an assisted one is the difference between starting
the trip and enduring it.

This guide explains how arrivals actually work at Noi Bai, who genuinely benefits from
fast-track assistance, and what to have ready either way.

---

## 1. The Terminal Layout, and the One Thing People Get Wrong

**All international commercial flights arrive at Terminal 2 (T2)**, the modern international
terminal. Domestic flights operate from **Terminal 1**, and this is where the most common
Noi Bai mistake happens.

T1 and T2 are **about 1 km apart**. They are not a walk across a concourse — there is a
**free orange shuttle bus** between them. Travellers who book a tight domestic connection
after a long-haul arrival, without accounting for immigration, baggage and the inter-terminal
transfer, are the ones sprinting.

If you have an onward domestic flight, the sequence is fixed and unavoidable:

1. Clear immigration at T2
2. Collect your baggage
3. Take the free orange shuttle to T1
4. Check in again for the domestic leg

Budget generously. This is a transfer, not a connection.

---

## 2. What the Arrival Sequence Looks Like

| Step | Where | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Disembark | T2 | Follow international arrivals signage to immigration |
| 2. Immigration | T2 | Passport + **printed eVisa on A4**, facial recognition scan |
| 3. Baggage claim | T2 | Collect checked luggage |
| 4. Customs | T2 | Green channel (nothing to declare) or red channel (goods to declare) |
| 5. Arrivals hall | T2 | Currency exchange, SIM/eSIM counters, ground transport |

The variable in that sequence is step 2. Immigration processing time depends on how many
wide-body aircraft landed in the same window, not on how organised you are.

## 3. Who Actually Needs Fast-Track

Fast-track assistance is a meet-and-greet service: someone meets you airside, escorts you
through a priority lane at immigration, and hands you off to your transport. It is not a
government service and it does not change your visa status — it changes how long you stand in
line.

It earns its cost in five situations:

**Business travellers on the clock.** If a meeting, a factory visit or a contract signing is
scheduled for the same day, an unpredictable queue is a scheduling risk rather than an
inconvenience.

**Delegations and groups.** Moving eight people through immigration together, with someone
handling the paperwork questions, is materially faster than eight people queueing
independently.

**Families with small children.** After a long-haul flight, a queue with a toddler is its own
category of difficulty. Being escorted through changes the arrival entirely.

**Late-night and early-morning arrivals.** Many long-haul routings land at awkward hours. This
is precisely when tired travellers make document mistakes and when onward transport is
thinnest.

**Elderly travellers or anyone with mobility needs.** Distances inside a large terminal are
longer than they look on a map.

Who does **not** need it: a solo traveller landing midday with hand luggage, a flexible
schedule and a hotel booked for the night. Save the money.

## 4. Getting from Noi Bai into Hanoi

Once you are through, there are three practical options into the city:

| Option | Cost | Frequency / timing | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Express Bus 86** | ~**45,000 VND** (about **$1.80**) | Departs every **45 minutes** | Budget travellers, daytime, light luggage |
| **Airport taxi** | Metered | Always available | Standard arrivals |
| **Pre-booked chauffeur** | Agreed in advance | **35–45 minutes** door to door | Late arrivals, groups, first visits |

Bus 86 is genuinely good value and runs a high-quality service, but it operates to a schedule
and drops at fixed points. At 02:00 with three suitcases and a hotel in the Old Quarter, a
pre-booked car is a different proposition entirely.

## 5. What to Have Ready Before You Land

- **Passport valid at least 6 months** from your arrival date
- **The approved eVisa, printed on A4**, carried with the passport — not only on a phone
- **The entry port on your visa must say Noi Bai.** A visa naming a different airport is a
  problem you cannot solve at the counter
- **Your hotel address in Vietnamese**, screenshotted for the driver
- **Connectivity arranged in advance** if you plan to use a ride-hailing app on arrival
- **Some Vietnamese dong** for the first day

One timing rule that catches people out regardless of terminal: an eVisa carries a "valid
from" date and **you cannot enter before it**. A flight landing at 23:40 against a visa
starting the next day is a refused boarding at the departure airport, because the airline
carries the liability.

## 6. Planning the Application Around the Arrival

Standard eVisa processing runs about **3 to 5 business days**, and weekends and Vietnamese
public holidays are not counted. An application submitted on a Friday afternoon does not begin
review until Monday morning. Apply two to three weeks out and the whole category of
last-minute panic disappears.

The government fees are **US$25 for single entry** and **US$50 for multiple entry**, and they
are non-refundable if the application is refused. If your itinerary includes a side trip out
of Vietnam and back — a common pattern for business travellers covering several countries in
one swing — you need multiple entry, because a single-entry visa ends the moment you leave.

## 7. Frequently Asked Questions

**Which terminal do international flights use at Noi Bai?**
Terminal 2. Domestic flights use Terminal 1, roughly 1 km away, connected by a free orange
shuttle bus.

**How long does immigration take?**
It varies with how many aircraft land in the same window. Fast-track exists precisely because
that variability cannot be planned around.

**Is fast-track an official government service?**
No. It is a private meet-and-greet and escort service. It does not alter your visa, your
eligibility, or the officer's decision.

**Can I use fast-track if my eVisa has a problem?**
An escort can help you navigate the process, but a visa naming the wrong port of entry is not
something any service can override.

**Do I need to print the eVisa?**
Yes. Print it on A4 and keep it with your passport.

## 8. Arriving at 1am: A Realistic Plan

Long-haul routings into Hanoi frequently land between midnight and 04:00, and almost every
problem travellers report at Noi Bai traces back to that window rather than to the airport
itself.

What changes after midnight:

- **Bus 86 is not an option.** It runs to a schedule, and the small hours are not on it.
- **Ride-hailing supply thins**, and you need working mobile data before you can book at all.
- **The taxi rank operates**, but it is the moment when tired travellers accept whatever
  number they are given.
- **Your hotel may not have staffed reception**, which matters if you arrive at 03:00 without
  having told them.

The plan that removes all four problems: arrange the transfer before you fly, install an eSIM
before you fly, and message the hotel with your flight number and arrival time. None of that
costs anything except ten minutes at home.

---

## 9. A Note on Where Fast-Track Ends

It is worth being precise about what an escort service can and cannot do, because expectations
cause more complaints than the service does.

**It can:** meet you airside, take you through a priority immigration lane, help with forms
and questions, carry the coordination when a group is moving together, and hand you to your
driver.

**It cannot:** change an officer's decision, fix a visa that names the wrong port of entry,
substitute for a passport that is short of the six-month rule, or produce a printed eVisa you
did not bring. Those are all decided before you leave home.

That is the useful frame: fast-track buys back time, not eligibility. Get the documents right
first, then decide whether the time is worth paying for.
