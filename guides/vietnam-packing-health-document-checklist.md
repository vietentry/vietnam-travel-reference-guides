# Packing for Vietnam: The First-Timer's Essential Health, Clothing and Document Checklist (2026)

Most trips to Vietnam that go wrong do not go wrong in Vietnam. They go wrong at a check-in
desk six thousand kilometres away, because of something missed at home: a passport with five
months left instead of six, an eVisa that was never printed, a drone packed without a permit.

This checklist is organised the way a trip actually unfolds — documents first, because they
are the only category that can end the journey before it starts, then health, clothing,
electronics and money.

---

## 1. Documents — The Category That Ends Trips at Check-In

| Item | The rule that matters |
|---|---|
| **Passport** | Valid **at least 6 months from your arrival date** — counted forward from the day you enter Vietnam, not the day you booked |
| **Approved eVisa** | **Printed on A4**, carried with the passport. Not only on your phone |
| **Entry port** | The checkpoint on your visa must match where you actually arrive |
| **Travel insurance** | Cover that includes medical evacuation; keep the policy number accessible offline |
| **Onward ticket** | Airlines and immigration may ask for proof of onward travel |
| **Driving licence + IDP** | An International Driving Permit alongside your national licence, if you intend to drive |

**The six-month rule is not negotiable and it is not the airline being difficult.** Carriers
are fined if they board a passenger who is refused entry, so a passport with five months and
twenty-eight days is a denied boarding, not a discussion.

**Print the eVisa.** Border officers, and particularly land-gate officers, expect a paper
document. A phone battery is not a travel document.

## 2. A Countdown That Prevents Most Problems

| When | Do this |
|---|---|
| **6 weeks before** | Check passport expiry against your arrival date. Start any vaccination course — some need more than a single shot |
| **3 weeks before** | Apply for your eVisa if you need one. Standard processing runs about **3–5 business days**, and weekends and Vietnamese public holidays do not count |
| **1 week before** | Buy travel insurance. Confirm the entry port on the approved visa matches your ticket |
| **48 hours before** | Print the eVisa on A4. Screenshot your hotel address in Vietnamese. Download offline maps |

Applying three weeks out rather than three days out is the single highest-value habit on this
page. It converts a processing delay from a crisis into an inconvenience.

## 3. Health and Medical Preparation

Vietnam's food is one of the reasons to go, and street food is safe to enjoy — but a tropical
climate and unfamiliar cuisine make a small personal kit worth its weight.

**Medication and first aid:**

- Digestive remedies and rehydration salts — the most-used items in any traveller's kit
- Probiotics, if you normally take them
- Any prescription medication in **personal quantities, in original pharmacy packaging, with
  an English-language prescription**
- Basic wound care: plasters, antiseptic, blister treatment for walking days
- Paracetamol or your usual analgesic

**Tropical-specific:**

- **Insect repellent containing DEET.** Dengue is mosquito-borne and daytime-biting, so
  repellent is a daytime item, not just an evening one
- **High-factor sunscreen** — the sun is stronger than most northern-hemisphere visitors
  expect, especially on water
- After-sun and a wide-brimmed hat

**Vaccinations:** discuss with a travel clinic at least six weeks out, since some courses need
staging.

## 4. Clothing: Pack for Three Climates, Not One

Vietnam runs 1,650 km north to south and does not have one climate. The mistake is packing
for "tropical" and landing in Hanoi in January.

| Region | What to expect | Pack |
|---|---|---|
| **North (Hanoi, Sapa, Ha Giang)** | Genuinely cool winters; cold in the mountains | Layers, a warm mid-layer, a jacket for Sapa |
| **Centre (Hue, Da Nang, Hoi An)** | Hot, with a distinct rainy season | Light breathable clothing, a compact rain layer |
| **South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong, Phu Quoc)** | Hot and humid year-round, monsoon downpours | Lightest clothing, quick-dry fabrics |

**Two items nearly everyone under-packs:**

- **A compact umbrella or light rain shell.** Monsoon rain arrives fast and stops fast.
  Waiting it out is normal; being soaked for the afternoon is optional.
- **Modest cover for temples and pagodas** — shoulders and knees covered. This applies at
  religious sites and mausoleums, and a light scarf plus long trousers solves it without
  bulk.

**Footwear:** comfortable shoes you can walk a city in, sandals for the heat, and something
that survives rain. Old Town streets and cave floors are uneven.

## 5. Electronics and Connectivity

| Item | Note |
|---|---|
| **Universal travel adapter** | Vietnam uses a mix of socket types |
| **Power bank** | Keep it **under 100 Wh** and in carry-on — airline rule, not a Vietnamese one |
| **eSIM or tourist SIM** | Arrange before you fly if you plan to use ride-hailing on arrival |
| **Offline maps** | Downloaded before landing. Caves, mountain passes and some bays have no signal on any network |
| **Drone** | **Do not pack one casually** — drones may require permits or approval, and customs takes this seriously |

The drone point deserves emphasis because it is the item most likely to turn a smooth customs
walk into a long conversation.

## 6. Money and Customs Limits

Vietnam is largely a cash economy outside hotels and larger restaurants, but card acceptance
is growing in cities.

**What to carry:** a mix. Cards for hotels, malls, supermarkets and ride-hailing apps; cash in
Vietnamese dong for street food, markets, motorbike taxis and rural areas.

**Declaration thresholds you should know before you pack:**

- Foreign currency **exceeding US$5,000** (or equivalent) must be declared
- Vietnamese dong **exceeding 15,000,000 VND** must be declared

**Duty-free personal allowances**, if you are bringing anything in:

- Spirits over 20% ABV: up to 1.5 litres — **or** wine under 20% ABV: up to 2.0 litres —
  **or** beer: up to 3.0 litres
- Tobacco: 200 cigarettes **or** 250 grams of loose tobacco

At the customs channel you choose green (nothing to declare) or red (goods to declare).
Declaring is quick; being found not to have declared is not.

## 8. What You Can Safely Leave Behind

Over-packing is the other common error. You can buy laundry services on nearly every street
in Hoi An and Hanoi for very little, which means a fortnight's trip does not need a
fortnight's clothing. Toiletries, sunscreen and basic medicines are widely available in
pharmacies and convenience stores. Heavy guidebooks are redundant with offline maps. A hair
dryer is in the hotel room.

Pack light. You will buy things.

## 9. Packing by Trip Length

**A long weekend (3–4 days), one city.** Carry-on only is realistic. Two changes of clothing,
the document set, a rain layer, and the medical basics. Laundry is cheap enough that anything
more is dead weight.

**Ten days to two weeks, multiple regions.** The bracket most first-timers fall into, and the
one where the north–south climate spread bites. If your route includes both Hanoi in winter
and the Mekong, you are genuinely packing for two seasons: pack the warm layer, and accept
that it lives in the bag for half the trip.

**A month or more, slow travel.** Pack for one week and do laundry. Add a 30-day connectivity
plan, a spare pair of shoes for the rain, and a small dry bag if boats or caves are on the
itinerary.

## 10. Season by Season, in One Paragraph Each

**Winter (December–February).** Hanoi and the northern mountains are genuinely cold — Sapa can
be near freezing. The south stays warm and dry, and this is peak season there. Pack a real
jacket if you are going north.

**Spring (March–April).** Arguably the best all-round window: the north warms up, the centre
is dry, the south is not yet at its wettest. Light layers cover the whole country.

**Summer (May–August).** Hot everywhere, with heavy afternoon downpours in the north and
south. The centre is at its hottest. Quick-dry fabrics, sunscreen and a rain shell earn their
place. Typhoons are possible along the central coast late in this window.

**Autumn (September–November).** The centre gets its heaviest rain, sometimes with flooding in
Hoi An. The north is beautiful and mild. Check regional forecasts rather than a national one.

## 11. Frequently Asked Questions

**How much passport validity do I actually need?**
At least six months counted from your **arrival date**, not from your booking date. Renew
early if you are anywhere near the line.

**Can I show the eVisa on my phone instead of printing it?**
Print it. Airline staff and border officers — especially at land crossings — expect paper,
and it costs nothing to carry.

**Do I need vaccinations for Vietnam?**
Speak to a travel clinic about six weeks before departure, because some courses need staging.
Requirements depend on your route and medical history.

**Is street food safe?**
For most travellers, yes — and it is one of the reasons to visit. Carry rehydration salts and
a digestive remedy anyway; unfamiliar food is unfamiliar regardless of hygiene.

**Can I bring my drone?**
Not without checking. Drones may require permits or approval, and this is enforced at
customs. Confirm the current position before you pack it, not after.

**How much cash should I bring?**
Enough for the first day or two, then withdraw locally. Remember the declaration thresholds:
over US$5,000 in foreign currency or over 15,000,000 VND must be declared on arrival.
