# Complete Directory of Vietnam's International Border Checkpoints for eVisa Holders (2026)

If you are applying for a Vietnam eVisa, one field on the application form causes more
avoidable problems than any other: the **intended port of entry**. It is a dropdown, it looks
harmless, and it is binding. Choose the wrong checkpoint and you can be turned away at the
border with a perfectly valid visa in your hand.

This directory lists every international checkpoint currently designated for eVisa entry,
grouped by type, so you can match the name on your application to the place you will actually
arrive.

> **A note on the number.** You will find older articles claiming Vietnam has 33 eVisa
> checkpoints, split 8 airports / 16 land gates / 9 seaports. That count is out of date. The
> current designated list is **42 checkpoints — 13 international airports, 16 land border
> gates and 13 seaports**. This guide uses the current figures. Always confirm against the
> official portal before you submit, because the list is expanded from time to time.

---

## 1. Why the Port of Entry Field Matters

An approved Vietnam eVisa is not a general permission to enter the country. It authorises
entry **at the specific checkpoint you nominated**. Immigration officers check the port
printed on the visa against the border post where you present yourself.

The three situations that catch travellers out:

- **Connecting flights that change your arrival city.** You book Hanoi, the airline reroutes
  you through Ho Chi Minh City, and your visa says Noi Bai.
- **Overland arrivals.** You fly into Bangkok and plan to cross by bus at Moc Bai, but your
  eVisa nominates Tan Son Nhat airport.
- **Cruise passengers.** Your ship docks at a port that is not on your visa.

The fix is free and takes seconds: pick the checkpoint you will genuinely use, and if your
itinerary changes before you travel, correct it before departure rather than at the barrier.

## 5. How to Choose the Right Checkpoint

A short decision sequence that avoids nearly all port problems:

1. **Confirm the arrival city on your ticket**, not the city you booked. Reroutings are
   common on long-haul connections.
2. **Match the checkpoint name exactly** as it appears on the official list. "Saigon" is
   Tan Son Nhat; "Hue" by sea is Chan May; "Ha Long" is Hon Gai.
3. **For overland travel, pick the gate, then buy the bus ticket** — not the other way round.
4. **If your plan changes before departure**, correct the port while the application can
   still be amended. That is far cheaper than being refused boarding or turned back.
5. **Print the approved eVisa on A4** and carry it with your passport. Officers at land gates
   in particular expect a paper copy.

## 6. What Actually Happens at the Checkpoint

Knowing the sequence removes most of the anxiety, and it is broadly the same at every
designated port.

**At an airport.** You follow the international arrivals corridor to immigration control,
present your passport together with the printed eVisa, and complete a facial recognition
scan. The officer checks that the port on your visa matches the airport you are standing in,
that the passport details match the visa exactly, and that your arrival date is not earlier
than the visa's start date. You then collect baggage and choose the green or red customs
channel depending on whether you have anything to declare.

**At a land gate.** The process is split in two. You clear exit formalities on the
neighbouring country's side, walk or drive across, then present your passport and printed
eVisa at the Vietnamese post. Land gates are the most paper-oriented of the three types —
carry the A4 printout, not just a phone screenshot, and expect the crossing to take longer
than an airport when a bus arrives at the same time as you.

**At a seaport.** Cruise lines usually collect passports and handle a group clearance with
the port authority. Your job is to have nominated the correct port on the visa, because the
ship cannot change that for you.

## 7. The Timing Rule That Catches People Out

A Vietnam eVisa carries a "valid from" date, and it is not a suggestion. **You cannot enter
before that date**, even by a few hours. A flight that lands at 23:30 on the 4th against a
visa starting on the 5th is a refused boarding at the departure airport, because the airline
is liable if you are turned back.

Two habits prevent this entirely:

- Set the "valid from" date to the day you actually cross, and if a red-eye flight lands just
  before midnight, set it to the earlier date.
- Apply with enough margin. Standard processing runs about **3 to 5 business days**, and
  weekends and Vietnamese public holidays do not count. An application submitted on a Friday
  afternoon does not begin review until Monday morning.

Note also that a **single-entry** visa ends the moment you leave Vietnam. If your plan
includes a side trip to Cambodia or Laos and a return, you need a multiple-entry visa. The
official government fees are **US$25 for single entry** and **US$50 for multiple entry**, and
they are non-refundable if the application is refused.

## 8. Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I enter through a checkpoint that is not on my visa?**
No. Plan to arrive at the nominated port, or correct the application before you travel.

**My connection changed and I now land in a different city. What do I do?**
Amend the application before departure if it has not yet been approved, or contact the
issuing channel about a correction. Do not fly and hope — the airline checks this at the gate.

**Is the port list the same for every nationality?**
The designated checkpoints are the same. What differs by nationality is whether you need an
eVisa at all, since some passports qualify for visa exemption for short stays.

**Do I need a paper copy?**
Yes, print it on A4. Land gates in particular expect paper, and a phone battery is not a
document.

**Does the list ever change?**
Yes. Vietnam has expanded the designated list before — which is exactly why older articles
still quote 33 checkpoints. Verify against the official portal on the day you apply.
