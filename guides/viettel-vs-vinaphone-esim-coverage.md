# Viettel vs Vinaphone eSIM: Which Network Gives Travellers the Best Coverage in Vietnam? (2026)

Vietnam is an easy country to stay connected in. Mobile data is fast, widely available and
inexpensive by regional standards, and the three major carriers — **Viettel, Vinaphone and
Mobifone** — between them cover the cities, the coast, the deltas and most of the mountains.

The question travellers actually face is narrower: **which network should you put on your
eSIM before you fly?** This guide compares the two largest, Viettel and Vinaphone, on the
terms that matter for a trip rather than for a phone contract.

---

## 1. eSIM or Physical SIM First

Before choosing a network, choose a format, because it changes what you can do on arrival.

| | **Tourist eSIM** | **Physical SIM at the airport** |
|---|---|---|
| Setup | Scan a QR code, typically before you fly | Find a counter, show your passport, staff install it |
| Delivery | QR code by email, instantly | In person, after you land |
| Your home number | Stays active in the phone for bank OTP codes | You remove your home SIM and store it |
| Working on landing | Yes — connects as you switch off flight mode | No — only after you reach the counter |
| Queueing | None | Whatever the arrivals hall is like that day |
| Requirement | A phone that supports eSIM and is unlocked | Most unlocked phones |

The practical argument for an eSIM is not the price, it is the **first hour**. Ride-hailing
apps, maps and your hotel's messaging all assume you are online the moment you clear customs.
An eSIM activated before departure means you walk out already connected. The counterargument
is real too: not every handset supports eSIM, and some older or region-locked phones do not.

## 2. Viettel: The Coverage Network

Viettel is the largest operator in the country and the one whose footprint extends furthest
from the cities. If your itinerary leaves the main corridors, this is the default choice.

**Where it earns its reputation:**

- **Northern mountains.** Sapa, the Ha Giang Loop, the passes of the north-west — this is
  where coverage differences stop being theoretical. Trekkers and motorbike travellers
  consistently report the strongest signal here.
- **Remote coastline and islands.** Smaller islands and less-developed stretches of coast
  where other networks thin out.
- **Rural deltas and inland provinces**, away from the tourist triangle.

**Choose Viettel if:** you are riding the Ha Giang Loop, trekking around Sapa, island-hopping
beyond Phu Quoc, or travelling anywhere your route is drawn on a map rather than a metro line.

---

## 3. Vinaphone: The Urban Performance Network

Vinaphone is the other major national carrier, and in the places most visitors actually spend
their time it is entirely competitive.

**Where it performs:**

- **Major cities.** Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang, Hue, Nha Trang — dense 4G/5G, and the
  practical difference against Viettel in a city centre is small.
- **The main tourist corridor.** The coastal route between the big three cities.
- **Airports and transport hubs**, where you first need data to work.

**Choose Vinaphone if:** your trip is cities, beaches and the coastal corridor, and you would
rather compare on plan value than on mountain coverage you will not use.

## 5. Plan Length: Match It to the Trip

Tourist data packages are sold by duration rather than by contract, which makes the decision
simple:

- **Short city break (3–7 days).** A short package covers it. You are on hotel and café Wi-Fi
  much of the time anyway; data is for maps and ride-hailing between them.
- **Standard two-week trip.** The most common bracket, and where daily-allowance plans make
  sense.
- **A month or longer.** A **30-day daily data plan** is the practical option for slow travel,
  digital nomads and anyone based in one city with weekend trips out.

Read the small print on "unlimited": many plans give a **generous full-speed daily allowance
and then reduce speed** for the rest of the day rather than cutting you off. For maps and
messaging that throttled tier is usually still usable; for video calls it is not.

## 6. Setting Up Before You Fly

1. **Check your phone supports eSIM** and is carrier-unlocked. This is the one step that
   cannot be fixed on arrival.
2. **Buy the plan and receive the QR code by email.** Save the email offline — you will need
   it in an airport with no data.
3. **Install the eSIM while you still have Wi-Fi at home.** Installation needs a connection;
   activation does not have to happen immediately.
4. **Keep your home SIM in the phone.** This is the quiet advantage of eSIM: your bank's OTP
   codes keep arriving on your usual number while data runs on the Vietnamese line.
5. **Switch data to the Vietnamese line on landing**, and turn off data roaming on the home line.

## 7. Which One Should You Actually Pick?

If you want one sentence: **take Viettel if your route leaves the cities, take Vinaphone if
it does not, and do not agonise over it** — in Hanoi, Da Nang or Ho Chi Minh City you will
struggle to tell them apart.

Two things matter more than the network choice:

- **Arrange it before you fly.** The difference between arriving connected and queueing at a
  counter is larger than any difference between the two carriers.
- **Keep your home number reachable.** Travellers who swap out their physical SIM and then
  cannot receive a bank verification code discover the cost of that at the worst moment.

## 8. Coverage by Region: What to Expect

A quick map of where connectivity is effortless and where it is worth thinking about.

| Region | What to expect | Network note |
|---|---|---|
| Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City, Da Nang | Dense 4G/5G, fast throughout | Either carrier is fine |
| Coastal corridor (Hue, Hoi An, Nha Trang) | Strong along the highway and in towns | Either carrier is fine |
| Mekong Delta | Good in towns, patchier on the water | Viettel edges ahead on boat trips |
| Sapa & the north-west | Good in town, variable on trekking routes | Viettel preferred |
| Ha Giang Loop | Genuinely remote in stretches | Viettel preferred |
| Phu Quoc & larger islands | Good in developed areas | Either, Viettel on the fringes |
| Cave and karst areas (Phong Nha, Ha Long) | Signal drops inside caves and on some bays | No carrier solves this — download offline maps |

The honest summary: for perhaps 85% of a normal itinerary the two networks are
indistinguishable. The remaining 15% is exactly where Viettel's reputation comes from.

## 9. Mistakes That Cost Travellers Their Connection

- **Buying an eSIM for a phone that does not support one.** Check first; it is the only
  unrecoverable error on this list.
- **Removing the home SIM to fit a physical Vietnamese one**, then discovering the bank sends
  verification codes to that number. If you must swap, enable an alternative verification
  method before you fly.
- **Waiting until arrival to buy data, then needing data to buy it.** Airport Wi-Fi usually
  bridges the gap, but not always, and not quickly.
- **Assuming "unlimited" means unlimited at full speed.** Read the daily allowance.
- **Leaving home data roaming switched on** alongside the Vietnamese line, and returning to a
  roaming bill that dwarfs the eSIM.
- **Not downloading offline maps.** Caves, remote passes and some bays have no signal from any
  carrier.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I keep my home number active while using a Vietnamese eSIM?**
Yes — that is the main advantage of eSIM over swapping the physical card. Keep the home line
for calls and OTP codes, run data on the Vietnamese line.

**Do I need to register with my passport?**
Physical tourist SIMs are registered at the counter with your passport. eSIM packages sold to
travellers are delivered by QR code without a counter visit.

**Will 5G work on my phone?**
Only if the handset supports the local 5G bands and you are in a covered area. On a 4G phone
the plan still works, at 4G speeds.

**Is the eSIM a visa or travel document?**
No. Connectivity has nothing to do with entry permission — you still need the correct visa or
exemption, and a printed eVisa if that is your route in.

**How early can I install the eSIM?**
Install it at home over Wi-Fi. Most travel plans start counting from activation or first
connection rather than from purchase, but check the terms of the specific package.
